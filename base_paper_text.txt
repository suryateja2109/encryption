Deepface-Based Chaotic Image Encryption Using
Key Optimization and Semi-Tensor Product Theory
Dawei Ding , Member, IEEE, Dong Xie , Hongwei Zhang , Zongli Yang, and Chu’an Liu

Abstract— Currently, protecting personal privacy through
selective encryption of facial images has become a research
hotspot. This paper aims to design a new image encryption
scheme using chaotic systems, optimization algorithms, and the
Semi-Tensor Product (STP) theory. Firstly, we proposed a 3D
Coupled Ikeda Map with Bounded Amplitude (3D-CIMBA),
which has controllable Lyapunov exponents and high-complexity.
Secondly, Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) algorithm is used
to generate control keys of the chaotic system, and produce
chaotic sequences. Then, DeepFace model is applied to recognize facial regions for encryption. Moreover, the face image is
encrypted by performing row-column alternation cyclic shifting
operation and STP diffusion. Finally, cryptographic analysis
is conducted using histograms, pixel correlation, information
entropy, and SSIM. The simulation results show that this scheme
demonstrates robustness against differential attacks and noise
attacks, which also exhibits fast encryption speed and large
key space. These results show that the proposed algorithm
can encrypt facial information more efficiently and securely
compared to traditional algorithms.
Index Terms— Chaotic system, image encryption, PSO optimization, DeepFace, STP theory.

more stringent requirements on encryption schemes. Many
scholars are dedicated to finding encryption schemes which are
more efficient and less vulnerable to attacks than traditional
solutions [5]. Lots of techniques, such as DNA encoding [6],
[7], compressive sensing [8], [9], [10] and information hiding [11], [12], [13], have been developed and are widely
combined with image encryption.

Since the concept of chaos was first proposed in 1963,
chaos theory has developed rapidly, gradually evolving into
a mature and practical theoretical framework [14]. Chaos
systems exhibit unpredictability and sensitivity to initial conditions [15], which means that the long-term iterative results
of chaotic systems are unpredictable, and small changes in
the initial state can lead to drastic changes in their dynamic
behavior [16], [17]. These characteristics makes these systems
widely used in the field of secure communication, especially
in image encryption. According to the definition of chaotic
system, it can be divided into two types: discrete systems and
continuous systems. Continuous systems, such as the Chen
system and Lorenz system, take longer for iterative solving,
whereas discrete systems have an advantage in terms of
computational cost. In recent years, many encryption schemes
based on discrete chaotic systems have been proposed [18].
Zhu et al. [19] proposed a 2D Logistic-modulated-SineCoupling-Logistic chaotic map (2D-LSMCL) to generate
chaotic matrices in image encryption. Qiu et al. [20] used
a hyperchaotic map 2D-CSCM to design an encryption
algorithm based on Rubik’s Cube scrambling. Kocak et al.
[21] proposed a new PSO key optimization algorithm based
on the Modular Integrated Logistic Exponential (MILE) map.
However, many existing chaotic image encryption schemes
have some short-comings [22]. For example, some of the
chaotic systems are insufficient in chaotic properties, having
simple structures and small range of system parameters, which
may lead to chaos degradation [23]. Ding et al. [24] introduced
the concept of controllable Lyapunov exponents, and by using
a modulo function, they ensured that the system parameters
exhibit chaotic behavior over a wide range. In addition, traditional encryption methods consist of two steps: scrambling
and diffusion [25]. The XOR pixel diffusion scheme is widely
used, making it susceptible to targeted attacks. Therefore,
many researchers are seeking new pixel confusion schemes
that differ from traditional methods, such as STP diffusion.
Gao et al. [26] used STP theory and a new map called
2D-LAIC to encrypt the 3D model. Gao et al. [27] used
Histogram of Oriented Gradients (HOG) to detect human face
and introduced STP diffusion in facial area encryption.
Most conventional encryption schemes with good results
have high time complexity. The Region of Interest (ROI)
encryption compensates for the shortcomings of the aforementioned time cost [28]. Shen et al. [29] proposed an optical
selective encryption scheme which was adapted in the ROI of
medical image. Jamel et al. [30] introduced ROI encryption

B. PSO Algorithm
Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) is a population-based
metaheuristic algorithm that mimics the social behavior of bird
flocking.
The basic idea of the PSO algorithm is to randomly initialize
a swarm of particles in the search space, with each particle
representing a point in the search space. Each particle has
two attributes: position and velocity. The position represents a
potential solution, while the velocity determines the particle’s
movement direction and distance [36]. The particles update
their positions by tracking two ‘best’ solutions: one is the
best position the particle has experienced (individual best
solution, pbest), and the other is the best position experienced
by the entire swarm (global best solution, gbest). During the
execution of the algorithm, all particles in each generation
update their velocities and positions according to the following
formula [37]:

C. Bifurcation Diagram
A bifurcation diagram is a visual representation used in
the study of dynamical systems, including chaotic systems,
to illustrate how the behavior of the system changes as a
parameter within the system is varied. These diagrams show
how a system transitions from simple, predictable behavior to
complex, chaotic dynamics through a series of bifurcations.
A bifurcation occurs when a small change in the parameter
value causes a sudden qualitative change in the behavior of
the system. Fig. 2(a)-(b) demonstrates the bifurcation diagram of classic Ikeda map’s 2 different parameters, while
Fig. 2(c)-(d) shows that of 3D-CIMBA. Similarly, we compared other classic three-dimensional mapping models, such
as VL-DMSIN [40]. We plotted the bifurcation diagrams for
parameters b0 and b2 separately, as shown in Fig. 2(e)-(h).
It can be observed from the figures that the proposed system
has a much bigger chaotic parameters range than the Ikeda
map.

B. Face Recognition and Database Matching
Our facial test set comes from the Internet Movie Database
(IMDB) [27]. Due to the large overall volume of the files,
3,000 images from IMDB.0 is used, with a total size of 1.3GB,
to construct the facial database. Additionally, 5 images not in
the database is selected for recognition testing, with the test
results presented in Section VI.
In DeepFace model, whether two images belong to the same
person is determined by comparing the Euclidean distance
between them and a preset threshold.
If the distance between two facial feature vectors is less than
this threshold, two images show the same person; otherwise,
they are different ones. This threshold is usually determined by
conducting experiments on a certain dataset and based on the
accuracy and recall rate of face verification. Different datasets
and application scenarios may require different threshold settings. In our simulation we set threshold of Euclidean distance
at 0.5. If the comparison is successful, the image would be
encrypted.

VI. C ONCLUSION
This paper proposes a new 3D chaotic system and designs
a new selective encryption scheme for color images. Firstly,
we use a PSO algorithm associated with the plaintext image
to search for the optimal key. Then, we use DeepFace to
achieve facial region recognition and extraction. The encryption of facial images is divided into two steps: a row-column
alternation cyclic shift of the image pixel positions and the
STP diffusion. Images’ information entropy, histogram, correlation and SSIM show that the encrypted image exhibits
a random distribution in space. Meanwhile, the encryption
system exhibit resistance to different attacks. Also, our scheme
achieves a shorter time consumption when encrypting the
same amount of information. In future work, we plan to
further modify the optimization algorithm to improve its
optimization iteration efficiency, redesign the algorithm, and
apply it to video encryption. Finally, we hope to implement
the lightweight deployment of our overall encryption process
and its application in secure transmission using the FPGA
platform.
